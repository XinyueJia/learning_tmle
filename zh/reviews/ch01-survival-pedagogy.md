## Findings on the learning sequence

- **The clinical coding comes before the probability calculation.** First dose defines time zero; capital T is the first progression or all-cause death time. The indicator explanation makes Y=1{T≤12} a concrete coding rule, including events at month 12. Death is already part of the composite endpoint and each person contributes at most once. Y=0 requires confirmed absence of the endpoint through the window. The month-8 loss example leaves Y unknown and explicitly forbids filling it with zero. Complete observation is a model assumption, not retrospective selection of completers.
- **The same sample is used throughout.** The opening 200 records, 84 zeros and 116 ones lead to 116/200=.58. The sample calculation precedes the probability expectation. P, P_n, μ and μ̂ are distinguished, including why the chosen teaching probabilities happen to equal the empirical frequencies. The whole-sample observed risk is repeatedly distinguished from A-minus-B ATE. X and A remain in the CSV for later chapters without becoming unexplained inputs to this marginal risk calculation.
- **The perturbation has a usable physical interpretation.** Each class retains 90% of its old probability before the extra 10% is added to the selected class. The prose and figures preserve both components of the new event probability. Fractional quantities are described as weights rather than fractional patients. Lower-case t has no month unit; capital T records time. The experiment labels repeat this distinction.
- **The algebra is genuinely retained.** Sections 2.1–2.3 show numerical distribution, grouping, the general indexed sum, separation of the three sums, extraction of a shared t, the indicator sum for both permitted z values, cancellation of μ−μ, and cancellation of t only when t≠0. The derivation does not ask the reader to infer these steps from the final formula. The elementary explanation of the non-zero difference quotient and its limit remains in the main line.
- **The function and its random input are introduced separately.** Section 3.1 first builds the two-input lookup table; 3.2 names the already-derived contamination rate as D(z;P); 3.3 puts random Y through the fixed rule. The semicolon is explained. Signed influence values are not treated as probabilities, changes to a patient's recorded outcome, or individual causal effects.
- **Centring is explained by a weighted ledger.** The numerical cancellation precedes the general proof. The unweighted average −.08 is an explicit counterexample to dividing by two, and the general derivation explains why the fixed mean can leave the sum. Section 3.5 preserves the old D while changing its averaging weights, producing .042 rather than silently recentering it. The zero-function counterexample prevents the mistaken rule that mean zero alone identifies an IF.
- **The survival connection stays within its data and model.** The fourth step derives S(12)=P(T>12)=E[1−Y]=1−μ=.42, then subtracts the new and old complements and obtains the negative IF through the same difference quotient. It explains the favourable/unfavourable direction of the two complementary targets. It does not claim an overall-survival endpoint, median survival, hazard ratio, full curve or censored-survival EIF from this binary table.
- **The Chapter 2 handover preserves the distinctions.** Chapter 2 continues with (.42,.58), explains absolute versus relative changes, and explicitly separates path score S from both survival S(12) and propensity score g. Its restoration of the eight joint cells is identified as a different perturbation from the two-class Y direction. The one-dimensional binary direction is not presented as a new independent direction merely because its speed changes.
